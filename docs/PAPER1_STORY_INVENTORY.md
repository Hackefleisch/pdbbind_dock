### 0.2 Protocol facts that must appear in Methods (verified in code)

- **Score function:** `ligand` weights with `fa_intra_rep 0.004`, `fa_elec 0.42`,
  `hbond_bb_sc`/`hbond_sc 1.3`, `rama 0.2`, `-restore_pre_talaris_2013_behavior`;
  `coordinate_constraint` weight 0.5 is added in `runner.py`.
- **Transform:** box 20 Å, 500 cycles, 0.1 Å / 5°, T = 5. **Perturb = `initial_perturb="5.0"`,
  Standard = no initial perturbation**, i.e. Standard starts exactly at the crystal ligand
  placement in all three receptor states. HighResDocker 1 cycle; FinalMinimizer with Cα
  restraints (0.3) within 7 Å; InterfaceScoreCalculator on chain X.
- **Conformers:** ETKDGv3, seed 12345; 49 / 199 / 299 generated by rotatable-bond count **plus the
  input (crystal) conformer, which is kept and loaded into the Rosetta rotamer library**
  (`generate_conformers` → `add_confs_to_res`). The thesis's 50/200/300 is the same count. This is a
  leak relative to the PoseBusters baselines (they start from an RDKit conformer) and must be
  disclosed; see S1.
- **Receptor states:** 10 FastRelax seeds each, backbone coordinate constraints kept on, no
  ramp-down; docking starts from the lowest-total-score seed. `apo_relax` = holo receptor relaxed
  with the ligand 500 Å away, ligand moved back afterwards. Never call it apo.
- **Stored `total_score` contains constraint energy (schema-1 files = all existing data).**
  Verified two ways on 2026-10-15: (i) `total_score` equals Σ wᵢ·rawᵢ only when the
  coordinate-constraint term is included (residual 4e-4 vs. up to 220 REU without it, 300 random
  PDBbind files); (ii) rescoring the stored structures without constraints reproduces
  `total_score − 0.5·raw_coordinate_constraint` to within coordinate rounding. Mechanism: `runner.py`
  removed the FastRelax restraints only *after* scoring the seeds, and Rosetta's FinalMinimizer keeps
  the Cα restraints it adds for the backbone minimisation (harmonic, σ = 0.3 Å, interface residues)
  unless the XML sets `remove_constraints="true"`. Magnitudes (raw units, ×0.5 = REU): seeds median
  ≈ 65 (up to 310); docked poses median ≈ 1–4, 90th percentile ≈ 2–12, max ≈ 440. The
  lowest-total-score seed chosen as docking reference would differ in 29 % of complexes without the
  term; `idelta_score` is unaffected. **Decision 2026-10-15: no re-docking, the docking code stays
  as it is.** The correction is applied on access: `Result(autocorrect=True)` (default) rewrites
  `total_score` and keeps the stored value as `total_score_legacy`; the full-set extraction carries
  the same columns. (Also answers Paper 2's `[VERIFY]` on the constraint term.)
- **RMSD.** Rosetta's `RMSDMetric` values in the H5 files are in place (no superposition, which is
  right: translation counts as docking error) but **not symmetry-corrected**. `docking/ligand_rmsd.py`
  now provides the symmetry-corrected in-place RMSD (heavy-atom graph automorphisms, element labels
  only), validated against Rosetta's `automorphic_rmsd` to 1e-3 Å and ~1000× faster.
  `Result(autocorrect=True)` rewrites `rmsd_to_crystal` / `rmsd_to_input` with it (stored values kept
  as `*_legacy`) and the extraction tables carry the same columns; the runner is unchanged. Effect:
  mean reduction 0.03–0.3 Å depending on ligand symmetry; for a small symmetric ligand (184l) 208 of
  1,800 poses move below the 2 Å cutoff.
- **Cost:** ≈ 1–4 s per docked pose; ≈ 1–17 min per relax seed (10 + 10 seeds per complex, so the
  outline's "≈ 4.7 h preparation" is plausible; distribution `[FROM REPO]` not yet computed).

**What the tables say, in one breath each**

1. The Standard protocol is near-native by construction (two thirds of its poses are ≤ 2 Å). It is a
   positive control, not a benchmark result. The headline protocol is **relax · perturb**; the hard
   setting is **apo_relax · perturb**.
2. Sampling is strong but sparse: one Perturb pose in twenty is a hit, so the 300-sample budget is
   part of the result (success-vs-N above). At 150 poses (thesis budget) the analytic rate is 87 %,
   which explains why the thesis-era 77–78 % looked lower.
3. The sampling–scoring gap is ≈ 24 points on PDBbind and PoseBusters (94.7 → 70.6, 94.6 → 71.1)
   and 15 on Astex; Top-5 recovers about half of it. The scorer is far better than chance (random
   Top-1 would be ≈ 4 %), so the honest framing is "good but unreliable", not "fails".
4. Receptor state matters for ranking more than for sampling: relax ≈ crystal > apo_relax in
   sampling (94.7 / 94.7 / 87.3) but relax > crystal > apo_relax in Top-1 (70.6 / 64.3 / 51.4).
5. Total score is a poor ranker on the unrelaxed crystal receptor (Top-1 28.9 vs 64.3) and on par
   with interface delta on relaxed receptors; part of that is the constraint energy in `total_score`
   (§0.2). This settles Appendix B question 1.

## 1b. Full-set success rates (2026-10-15; symmetry-corrected RMSD, constraint-free totals)

Source: `data/docking_stats/<set>_full/` extracted through `Result(autocorrect=True)` (every H5 file;
the tables use the complete complexes: PDBbind 11,444 of 11,460 files,
PoseBusters 388 of 428, Astex 84 of
85), computed by `docking_analysis.benchmark`. CSVs, the per-complex tables and this
report (`success_tables_rmsd_to_crystal_2A.md`) are in `data/figures/benchmark/`. One table per starting
complex × protocol, rows are the benchmark sets; the six tables are given twice, ranked by interface
delta and by the corrected total score.

### Legend

**Hit.** A pose is a hit when `rmsd_to_crystal` ≤ 2 Å. `rmsd_to_crystal` is the symmetry-corrected, in-place (no superposition) heavy-atom RMSD of the docked ligand to the crystal ligand, as auto-corrected by `docking.result.Result`; the stored Rosetta value without symmetry correction is `rmsd_to_crystal_legacy`.

**Ranking score** (one family of tables per score; lower is better):
- `idelta_score` — Rosetta interface delta (energy of the complex minus energy of the separated partners; lower is better, not affected by the constraint issue)
- `total_score` — Rosetta total score with the coordinate-constraint energy removed (auto-corrected; the stored value is `total_score_legacy`); lower is better

**Starting complex** (receptor state the docking started from):
- `crystal` — the input complex as prepared (no relaxation)
- `relax` — lowest-scoring of 10 FastRelax runs of the complex with the ligand in place (backbone restrained)
- `apo_relax` — lowest-scoring of 10 FastRelax runs with the ligand moved 500 Å away, ligand placed back afterwards (a holo receptor relaxed without its ligand, not an apo structure; the placed-back ligand can be displaced)

**Protocol:**
- `std` — Transform/HighResDocker/FinalMinimizer starting at the input ligand placement (no initial perturbation)
- `perturb` — same protocol with an initial random perturbation of up to 5 Å (`initial_perturb="5.0"`)

**Columns** (percent of complexes unless stated; 300 poses per complex and combination):
- `complexes` — number of complexes of the set that contribute (complete runs: all 300 poses of this combination present)
- `poses_hit` — pose-level hit rate: percent of all poses of the combination (300 x complexes, pooled) that are hits
- `any_hit` — percent of complexes with at least one hit among their 300 poses — the sampling success, independent of any score
- `median_hit_frac` — median over complexes of the per-complex fraction of hits (percent) — how dense hits are in a typical run
- `top1` — percent of complexes whose best-ranked pose (by the ranking score) is a hit
- `top5` — percent of complexes with a hit among the 5 best-ranked poses
- `top10` — percent of complexes with a hit among the 10 best-ranked poses
- `N=k` — analytic sampling success: percent of complexes expected to have at least one hit among k samples, 1 - (1 - p)^k averaged over complexes, p = per-complex hit fraction (treats samples as independent)

**Reading the tables**

1. The two `std` protocols on the crystal and relaxed receptor are positive controls (two thirds of
   their poses are hits because they start at the crystal placement). The `apo_relax` · `std` table is
   weaker because the ligand-away relaxation moved the ligand: its reference ligand sits a median
   1.1 Å (39 % of PDBbind complexes > 2 Å) from the crystal pose, so that protocol does not start at
   the crystal placement either.
2. Headline setting *(PE, 2026-10-16)*: `apo_relax` · `perturb`, the hardest scenario and the clearest
   view of the scoring problem — any-hit 88.5 / 88.4 / 94.0 % against Top-1 53.1 / 57.5 / 64.3 %
   (PDBbind / PoseBusters / Astex). `relax` · `perturb` is the realistic holo setting: any-hit
   95.4 / 95.1 / 98.8 %, Top-1 72.0 / 72.9 / 85.7 %, Top-5 84.6 / 83.5 / 91.7 %, a gap of ≈ 23 points.
   Symmetry correction added 1–3 points everywhere. The interpretation of all six settings is in S1.
3. One Perturb pose in ≈ 18 is a hit, so the budget matters: at N = 150 (the thesis budget) the
   analytic any-hit rate is 89 %, at 300 it is 93 %.
4. Interface delta vs. total score as the ranker (Top-1, interface delta / total score):

| combination | PDBbind | PoseBusters | Astex |
|---|---|---|---|
| `crystal` · `std` | 86.0 / 73.0 | 89.4 / 82.5 | 96.4 / 71.4 |

## 2. The stories, by track

The three tracks of the 2026-10-09 story update ([PAPER_OUTLINE.md](PAPER_OUTLINE.md)): **T1 Benchmark**
(how well does RosettaLigand work, and by which mode does it reach near-crystal poses), **T2 Fix the
ranking** (can the failure be repaired, and why does it resist), **T3 Affinity**. S2 is the bridge: a
benchmark result and the premise of T2. Interpretations added on 2026-10-16 are marked *(PE)*.

### Track T1 — Benchmark

#### S1 — RosettaLigand is a strong sampler, and the six settings show how it works

- **Claim.** A ≤ 2 Å pose is among 300 samples for ≈ 95 % of complexes with the perturbed start on the
  crystal or relaxed receptor, and for ≈ 88 % on the ligand-away receptor (§1b).
- **Headline setting *(PE)*: `apo_relax` · `perturb`.** It is the hardest scenario (receptor relaxed
  without its ligand, start displaced by up to 5 Å) and shows the scoring problem most clearly: any-hit
  88.5 / 88.4 / 94.0 % against Top-1 53.1 / 57.5 / 64.3 % (PDBbind / PoseBusters / Astex), a gap of
  30–35 points. `relax` · `perturb` is the realistic holo setting (95 % any-hit, 72 % Top-1) and
  `crystal` · `perturb` the unprepared one.
- **Ranking score *(PE)*: interface delta.** The tables give no strong performance reason to prefer
  either score (§1b comparison: within two points on relaxed receptors), but interface delta makes the
  evaluation independent of the structure quality, whereas the total score collapses on the unrelaxed
  crystal receptor (Top-1 29 vs 66 %). Interface delta is used throughout.
- **Relaxation before docking is not necessary *(PE)*.** The crystal receptor samples as well as the
  relaxed one (any-hit 95.3 vs 95.4 %) and ranks ≈ 5 points lower with interface delta (65.9 vs 72.0 %
  on PDBbind, 67.3 vs 72.9 % on PoseBusters, 81.0 vs 85.7 % on Astex). Relaxation is required only if
  the total score is to be used.
- **A third of the `std` trajectories leave the correct placement — and where they end up *(PE,
  refined 2026-10-16)*.** In the three `std` runs the ligand starts at (for `apo_relax`: close to) the
  crystal placement, yet only 66 / 66 / 36 % of the poses end within 2 Å. Two facts bound the
  interpretation. (i) The crystal placement *is* a full-atom minimum: during FastRelax of the holo
  complex the ligand stays within a median 0.26 Å of it (§1b note 1). (ii) The `std` protocol moves
  the ligand with the Transform random walk under the coarse grid score *before* any full-atom
  optimisation, so leaving happens in that walk, and the high-resolution stage then settles into the
  nearest basin. The poses that leave are mostly near-misses (median 3.5 Å; only 7 % beyond 5 Å) and
  score *worse* than the ones that stay: per complex the non-hits are on average 5–6 REU worse in
  interface delta, and they are better on average in only 2 % of complexes. So the picture is
  "hard to leave a basin, easy to be pushed into one" (S3): the walk pushes the ligand into a
  neighbouring, worse basin and the optimiser does not bring it back. The energy function's own
  failure is narrower but real: in 14 / 11 / 23 % of complexes (`crystal` / `relax` / `apo_relax`
  · `std`) the best-scoring non-hit beats the best hit, which is exactly the Top-1 loss of those
  settings.
- **The holo receptor carries a bias that can be removed *(PE)*.** The drop of the pose-level hit rate
  from 66 % (`relax` · `std`) to 36 % (`apo_relax` · `std`) shows how much the ligand-bound receptor
  conformation pre-organises the pocket, and that relaxing without the ligand removes that bias. This
  is what makes `apo_relax` interesting for cross-docking, which is not tested here. Part of the drop
  is mechanical and must be stated with the number: the placed-back ligand of the `apo_relax`
  reference is displaced by a median 1.1 Å (> 2 Å for 39 % of PDBbind complexes), so that `std` run
  does not start exactly at the crystal placement.
- **Why `apo_relax` · `perturb` ranks so much worse than it samples *(PE)*.** With noise in the start
  and the receptor bias removed, RosettaLigand samples a much more diverse set of states with equal
  energetic profiles; the any-hit rate falls only moderately (95 → 88 %) while Top-1 falls hard
  (72 → 53 %). This is the sampling-side view of the degeneracy that T2 quantifies (S6).
- **Sampling budget.** One Perturb pose in ≈ 18 is a hit; the analytic any-hit rate is 89 % at N = 150
  and 93 % at N = 300 for `relax` · `perturb`, 78 % and 84 % for `apo_relax` · `perturb`.
- **Caveats that must travel with the claims:** re-docking into the known pocket with a ≤ 5 Å start;
  the crystal conformer is in the rotamer library (§0.2); the receptor is holo-derived in all three
  states; `apo_relax` is not an apo structure.

  - Decision + `[COMPUTE]`: Appendix C run **A** (random starts in a pocket box, crystal conformer
    excluded) if the comparison with DL / Vina / Gold is to be made head-on; start on Astex +
    PoseBusters. The abstract's "often beating recent deep-learning methods" rests on this.
  - Literature `[CITE]`: the PoseBusters paper's baseline numbers and their exact conditions.

#### S2 — The score does not reliably pick the pose it found (bridge to T2)

- **Claim.** Top-1 by interface delta sits 23 points below the any-hit rate for `relax` · `perturb` and
  30–35 points below it for `apo_relax` · `perturb`; Top-5 closes about half of the gap.
- **Can tell now:** §1b, both ranking scores.
- **Interpretation *(PE)*.** The gap widens exactly when the start is noisy and the receptor bias is
  removed, i.e. when the sampler produces many distinct states of equal energetic profile. The score
  is still far better than chance (random Top-1 would be ≈ 4 %), so the framing is "good but
  unreliable". The `std` runs quantify the two parts (S1): wrong neighbouring states usually score
  worse than the correct basin (non-hits 5–6 REU worse on average), but in 11–23 % of complexes a
  wrong state scores better than every correct one.
- **To do (small):** rank-of-first-hit distribution per complex (plotted as density per rank); fraction of complexes whose Top-1 is
  a > [0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8] Å decoy (feeds S6); one figure per setting.

#### S3 — The starting position decides what RosettaLigand finds (Josephine Eboh, external study)

- **Provenance.** An independent student project run outside this repository; all inputs, runs and
  result files live in her project directory and none of it is in this repo. The findings are included
  because they fit the T1 question of *how* the sampler reaches the crystal basin.
- **Findings, reframed *(PE)*.**
  1. RosettaLigand depends on the quality of the starting position: success collapses as the starting
     RMSD grows (4j1p, minimised starts, no perturbation: 1.9 Å → 7 %, 3.1 Å → 5 %, ≥ 5.7 Å → 0 %).
  2. The initial perturbation decreases success from close-to-optimal starts but increases it from
     bad ones (4j1p, minimised starts: 7.3 → 1.3 % at 1.9 Å; 0 → 0.7–3.3 % at 5.7–10.7 Å).
  3. Minimised versus unminimised starting points *(settled 2026-10-16)*: it is hard to leave a basin
     and easy to be pushed into one. From unminimised, clashing starts (generated without the
     high-resolution step; mean total score > 0) success is *higher* than from minimised starts at
     comparable RMSD (4j1p: 1.4–1.8 Å → 19–23 % vs 7 % at 1.9 Å; 3.6 Å → 17 % vs 5 % at 3.1 Å; 3sff
     has no close unminimised starts). A fully optimised start is a local minimum the run rarely
     leaves; a clashing start is pushed by the optimiser and lands in the correct basin more often;
     the perturbation's harm at close starts comes from leaving the basin, not from clashes. The
     benchmark's `std` runs show the same mechanism from the other side (S1): the Transform walk
     pushes a third of the ligands into neighbouring, worse-scoring basins, and they stay there.
- **Relation to the benchmark.** Findings 1–2 are the per-complex version of the `std` vs `perturb`
  comparison in §1b (a ≤ 5 Å displacement turns 66 % of hits into 6 %); finding 3 connects to the
  energy-degeneracy argument of T2 (many near-degenerate minima that the optimiser does not leave).
